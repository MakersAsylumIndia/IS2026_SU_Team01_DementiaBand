**What is Dementia?**

Dementia is an umbrella term for a decline in cognitive function severe enough to interfere with daily life. It is not a single disease but a syndrome caused by various underlying conditions that damage brain cells, affecting memory, thinking, and behavior.

**Causes:**

Dementia is caused by physical damage to brain cells, which prevents them from communicating normally. Specific root causes include: 

- **Alzheimer’s Disease:** The most common type, caused by the buildup of abnormal protein "tangles" and "plaques" in the brain.

- **Vascular Dementia:** Caused by blocked or damaged blood vessels in the brain, often resulting from strokes or atherosclerosis.

- **Lewy Body Dementia (LBD):** Driven by abnormal protein deposits (Lewy bodies) that damage nerve cells.

- **Frontotemporal Dementia (FTD):** Results from the progressive damage of the frontal and temporal lobes of the brain.

- **Other Conditions:** Parkinson's disease, traumatic brain injury, and treatable issues like severe vitamin B-12 deficiency or thyroid problems.


**Symptoms:**

Symptoms typically progress over time, often starting subtly and worsening until individuals require assistance with daily activities. 

- **Cognitive:** Memory loss (especially recent events), difficulty finding words, confusion with time or place, and trouble completing familiar, multi-step tasks like managing money.

- **Psychological & Behavioral:** Personality changes, inappropriate social behavior, anxiety, depression, and withdrawal from work or social activities.

- **Physical:** Wandering, getting lost in familiar neighborhoods, and balance or movement issues.





**Treatment:**

While most progressive forms of dementia cannot be cured, treatments exist to manage symptoms, slow progression, and improve quality of life. 

  - Occupational therapy to adapt the home environment for safety.

  - Cognitive stimulation activities to keep the brain active.

  - Support groups and counseling for both the patient and caregivers



The ‘WHY’ behind our project: Wandering


Patients with dementia often exhibit a **tendency to wander**, which is one of the most significant safety concerns faced by caregivers and healthcare providers. 

Studies indicate that approximately **60% of people with dementia will wander at least once during the course of their illness**, with the risk increasing as cognitive impairment progresses Research has also shown that if a missing dementia patient is not found within 24 hours, the likelihood of serious injury or death rises significantly.


**Why Do Dementia Patients Wander?**

- **Memory loss and confusion:** Patients may forget where they are or where they intended to go. 

- **Disorientation:** Difficulty recognizing familiar places or understanding time and location. 

- **Restlessness and anxiety:** Feelings of stress, boredom, or agitation can trigger movement. 

- **Searching for something familiar:** Patients may believe they need to go to work, pick up children, or return to a previous home. 

- **Physical activity needs:** Some individuals walk to satisfy an urge for movement or exercise. 

- **Environmental triggers:** Noise, crowds, changes in routine, or unfamiliar surroundings may cause confusion and wandering. 




**Risks Associated with Wandering:**

- Falls and physical injuries 

- Traffic accidents 

- Exposure to extreme weather conditions 

- Dehydration and exhaustion 

- Getting lost in unfamiliar areas 

- Increased emotional distress for both patients and caregivers
